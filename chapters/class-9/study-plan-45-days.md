## Class 9 Mathematics: 45-Day Plan

This plan is built for **90 minutes a day**, with a 60-minute version for busy days and a 120-minute version when a topic needs more practice. The aim is not to finish quickly. The aim is to recognise the question, recall the method, and write a clear answer within examination time.

> **Guiding rule:** Understand first, remember second, apply third. A formula memorised without understanding disappears under examination pressure.

## 1. The Daily 90-Minute Routine

| Time | Step | What she does |
|---|---|---|
| 5 min | Recall | Explain yesterday's formula or solve two quick questions without looking. |
| 15 min | Understand | Read one small concept and explain it aloud in her own words. |
| 20 min | Practise together | Work through one or two examples with a parent or the guide. |
| 20 min | Independent practice | Solve similar questions alone, with the guide closed. |
| 10 min | Check and correct | Compare with the answers, mark the first wrong step, and write it in the mistakes notebook. |
| 10 min | Application | Try one word problem or real-life question using the same idea. |
| 10 min | Remember | Close the book and write the formula and method from memory. |

On a 60-minute day, keep recall, understanding, independent practice and the memory check, and reduce the number of practice questions. On a 120-minute day, add more problems and corrections.

<div class="formula-box">

**Parent tip:** Ask "Can you explain why?" before asking "Did you get the answer?" A correct answer reached by a lucky guess is not yet mastery.

</div>

## 2. The Three Phases

| Phase | Days | Focus | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Repair | 1–7 | Diagnostic and all eight foundation lessons | Find and fix gaps that block Class 9 work. |
| 2. Build | 8–38 | All 14 Ganita Manjari chapters | Learn each chapter, then practise it. |
| 3. Perform | 39–45 | Revision, mock paper, final recall | Turn knowledge into examination marks. |

## 3. Phase 1: Repair the Foundations (Days 1–7)

| Day | Topic | She should be able to |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Diagnostic assessment | Attempt 20 short questions and identify weak skills. |
| 2 | Foundation 1: Numbers and Signs | Use the number line, place value, absolute value, signs and BODMAS. |
| 3 | Foundation 2: Fractions, Decimals and Percentages | Add, subtract, multiply and divide fractions; convert decimals and percentages. |
| 4 | Foundation 3 and 4: Ratio and Proportion; Powers and Roots | Share in a ratio, use proportion, apply index laws and find roots. |
| 5 | Foundation 5: Basic Algebra | Use variables, terms, brackets and simple equations. |
| 6 | Foundation 6 and 7: Algebraic Operations; Geometry Vocabulary | Expand and factorise; use angle facts and shape vocabulary. |
| 7 | Foundation 8: Measurement and Units | Convert units and use perimeter, area and volume formulas. |

These are **repair sessions**, not a full re-teaching of Classes 5–8. Only the gaps that block Class 9 work are revised. A strong student may finish several lessons in one sitting.

## 4. Phase 2: Build the 14 Chapters (Days 8–38)

| Days | Chapter | Main target |
|---|---|---|
| 8–9 | 1. Orienting Yourself: The Use of Coordinates | Plotting, quadrants, distance and midpoint. |
| 10–11 | 2. Introduction to Linear Polynomials | Terms, coefficients, degree, linear graphs and slope. |
| 12–14 | 3. The World of Numbers | Rational and irrational numbers, decimals, proofs and the square-root spiral. |
| 15–17 | 4. Exploring Algebraic Identities | Identities, expansion, factorisation and rational expressions. |
| 18–20 | 5. I'm Up and Down, and Round and Round | Circle vocabulary, chords, arcs and cyclic quadrilaterals. |
| 21–23 | 6. Measuring Space: Perimeter and Area | Perimeter, area, arc length, sectors, Heron's and Brahmagupta's formulas. |
| 24–25 | 7. The Mathematics of Maybe | Probability scale, experimental and theoretical probability, sample spaces. |
| 26–27 | 8. Predicting What Comes Next | Sequences, recursive and explicit rules, AP, GP and the sum of natural numbers. |
| 28–29 | 9. Propositions and their Converses | Statements, truth values, converses, counterexamples and reasoning. |
| 30–31 | 10. How Quantities Combine: Understanding Data | Mean, median, mode, weighted averages, mixtures and stacked bar charts. |
| 32 | 11. The World of Algorithms | Step-by-step algorithms, HCF and Euclid's subtraction method. |
| 33–35 | 12. Quadrilaterals | Properties, parallelogram theorems, midpoint theorem and symmetry. |
| 36–37 | 13. Two Variables, One Line | Linear equations, graphs, substitution and elimination. |
| 38 | 14. Math of Space: Surface Area and Volume | Cuboids, cubes, cylinders, cones, spheres and hemispheres. |

The time is deliberately uneven. Geometry, algebra and mensuration receive more days because they need more reasoning and practice.

## 5. Phase 3: Perform (Days 39–45)

| Day | Revision focus | Practice |
|---|---|---|
| 39 | Chapters 1–4 | Coordinates, polynomials, numbers and identities. |
| 40 | Chapters 5 and 12 | Circle theorems and quadrilateral proofs. |
| 41 | Chapters 6 and 14 | Mensuration formulas and calculations with units. |
| 42 | Chapters 7, 8, 10 and 11 | Probability, progressions, data and algorithms. |
| 43 | Chapters 9 and 13, plus weak areas | Reasoning and pairs of linear equations. |
| 44 | Full-length mock paper | Timed, written under examination conditions. |
| 45 | Final revision | Formula recall, mistakes notebook and selected easy-to-medium questions. |

> **Important:** The official CBSE unit weightage guides priorities, but it does not fix the exact number of questions per chapter. The school's blueprint and prescribed portions must be confirmed with the school.

## 6. Four Levels of Mastery

| Level | Expected ability | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Remember | Recall the formula or definition. | Writes it correctly from memory. |
| 2. Understand | Explain what it means and why it works. | Explains it aloud without the book. |
| 3. Apply | Solve a familiar question independently. | Correct answer with clear steps. |
| 4. Perform | Solve a new question accurately in time. | Correct method in a timed attempt. |

A topic is not mastered because she can follow a worked example. The milestone is solving a similar problem alone.

## 7. How to Write an Answer That Earns Marks

1. **Given:** write the information provided.
2. **Required:** state what must be found.
3. **Formula or theorem:** write it before substituting.
4. **Substitution:** insert the values.
5. **Calculation:** show the steps; do not jump to the answer.
6. **Answer:** state the result with units where needed.

| Subject | Examination habit |
|---|---|
| Algebra | Show intermediate steps and keep brackets until they are simplified. |
| Geometry | Draw and label the diagram; name the theorem or property used. |
| Mensuration | Write the formula, substitute, and use square or cubic units. |
| Probability | Write the total number of outcomes and the favourable outcomes. |
| Coordinate geometry | Write the coordinates clearly and state the formula. |

## 8. The Mistakes Notebook

For every wrong answer, record:

1. The question.
2. The first incorrect step.
3. Why the step was wrong.
4. The correct reasoning.
5. A similar question to try again after three days.

<div class="formula-box">

**Why this works:** Repeating questions she already knows feels comfortable but changes little. Correcting the exact step that went wrong removes the same mistake from the next test.

</div>

## 9. Weekly Confidence Check

At the end of each week, ask three questions:

- Which formula can I now write without looking?
- Which type of question can I now start on my own?
- Which single mistake will I remove next week?

Mark the answer honestly. Confidence grows from evidence, not from reassurance alone.
