# Number Systems: Full Solutions

Full worked solutions for the **Application Questions**, including diagrams where helpful.

## 1. Decimal to fraction

**Question:** Write $0.6$ as a fraction in simplest form.

**Method:** $0.6=\frac6{10}$. Cancel by HCF 2.

**Step 1 — Write over 10.**

$$
0.6=\frac6{10}
$$

**Step 2 — Cancel.**

$$
\frac{6\div2}{10\div2}=\frac35
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac35}
$$

## 2. Recurring decimal

**Question:** Write $0.\overline{7}$ as a fraction.

**Method:** Let $x=0.\overline{7}$. Multiply by 10 and subtract.

**Step 1 — Set up.**

$$
x=0.777\ldots,\qquad 10x=7.777\ldots
$$

**Step 2 — Subtract.**

$$
9x=7 \Rightarrow x=\frac79
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac79}
$$

## 3. Laws of exponents

**Question:** Simplify $5^2\times5^3$.

**Method:** Same base, add indices: $5^{2+3}=5^5$.

$$
5^5=3125
$$

$$
\boxed{5^5=3125}
$$

## 4. Laws of exponents

**Question:** Simplify $(3^2)^3$.

**Method:** Power of a power, multiply indices: $3^{2\times3}=3^6$.

$$
3^6=729
$$

$$
\boxed{3^6=729}
$$

## 5. Rational between two rationals

**Question:** Find a rational number between $\frac12$ and $\frac23$.

**Method:** Take the average: $\frac12\left(\frac12+\frac23\right)$.

**Step 1 — Add.**

$$
\frac12+\frac23=\frac36+\frac46=\frac76
$$

**Step 2 — Halve.**

$$
\frac12\times\frac76=\frac7{12}
$$

$$
\boxed{\frac7{12}}
$$

Check: $\frac12=\frac6{12}<\frac7{12}<\frac8{12}=\frac23$. ✓

## 6. Terminating or recurring

**Question:** Without dividing, state whether $\frac78$ has a terminating or recurring decimal expansion.

**Method:** A fraction in lowest terms has a terminating decimal expansion iff its denominator's prime factors are only 2 and/or 5. $8=2^3$, so it terminates.

**Step 1 — Factor denominator.**

$$
8=2^3
$$

Only prime factor is 2, so $\frac78$ terminates.

$$
\boxed{\text{Terminating }(=0.875)}
$$

## 7. Irrational between two rationals

**Question:** Find an irrational number between 1 and 2.

**Method:** The square root of a non-perfect-square between $1^2$ and $2^2$ is irrational. $\sqrt2\approx1.414$ lies between 1 and 2.

$$
\boxed{\sqrt2\text{ (about }1.414\text{)}}
$$

Any non-perfect-square root such as $\sqrt3$ also works.

## 8. Decimal expansion

**Question:** Write $\frac38$ as a decimal.

**Method:** Divide 3 by 8, or note $\frac38=\frac{375}{1000}$.

$$
\frac38=\frac{375}{1000}=0.375
$$

$$
\boxed{0.375}
$$

## 9. Simplify indices

**Question:** Simplify $\frac{7^5}{7^2}$.

**Method:** Same base, subtract indices: $7^{5-2}=7^3$.

$$
7^3=343
$$

$$
\boxed{7^3=343}
$$

## 10. Square root of a perfect square

**Question:** Find $\sqrt{144}$.

**Method:** Number squared giving 144: $12\times12=144$.

$$
\boxed{12}
$$

## 11. Estimate an irrational

**Question:** $\sqrt{10}$ lies between which two consecutive whole numbers?

**Method:** Find perfect squares around 10: $3^2=9<10<16=4^2$. So $3<\sqrt{10}<4$.

$$
\boxed{3\text{ and }4}
$$

## 12. Rational or irrational

**Question:** State whether $0.101001000100001\ldots$ is rational or irrational, and why.

**Method:** A rational number has a decimal expansion that either terminates or repeats with a fixed block. This decimal has a pattern of increasing zeros ($1$, then $00$, then $000$, ...), so the repeating block grows and never settles into a fixed cycle.

$$
\boxed{\text{Irrational — non-terminating, non-repeating decimal}}
$$
