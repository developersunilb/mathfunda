## Foundation 5: Basic Algebra

Algebra replaces unknown numbers with letters so that we can write a rule once and use it many times. This lesson repairs vocabulary, like terms and simple equations before the Class 9 algebra chapters.

## 1. Remember the Basics

### Variables, constants and coefficients

| Word | Meaning | Example in $5x+3$ |
|---|---|---|
| Variable | A letter representing a number that can change | $x$ |
| Constant | A fixed number | $3$ |
| Coefficient | The number multiplying a variable | $5$ |
| Term | A part separated by $+$ or $-$ signs | $5x$ and $3$ |
| Expression | A mathematical phrase with no equals sign | $5x+3$ |
| Equation | A statement that two expressions are equal | $5x+3=18$ |
| Identity | An equation true for every allowed value | $(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2$ |

> **Important distinction:** An expression is simplified; an equation is solved. An identity is proved and is true for all values.

### Terms and like terms

**Like terms** have exactly the same variable part, including the same powers.

- Like terms: $3x$ and $5x$; $\;2y^2$ and $-7y^2$
- Unlike terms: $3x$ and $3y$; $\;4x$ and $4x^2$

Only like terms can be combined. Add their coefficients and keep the variable part:

$$
3x+5x=8x
$$

### Writing multiplication correctly

| Written form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $3x$ | $3\times x$ |
| $xy$ | $x\times y$ |
| $x^2$ | $x\times x$ |
| $2(x+1)$ | $2\times(x+1)$ |

The multiplication sign is usually left out between a number and a letter, or between two letters.

### Evaluating an expression

To **evaluate**, substitute the given value and calculate carefully. Use brackets when the value is negative.

**Example:** Evaluate $2x+3$ for $x=4$.

$$
2(4)+3=8+3=11
$$

### Simple equations: the golden rule

An equation is balanced. Whatever operation you perform on one side, perform on the same side.

$$
3x+7=22
$$

Subtract $7$ from both sides:

$$
3x=15
$$

Divide both sides by $3$:

$$
x=5
$$

### Checking a solution

Substitute the answer back into the original equation:

$$
3(5)+7=15+7=22
$$

Since both sides are equal, $x=5$ is correct.

### Translating words into algebra

| Words | Expression |
|---|---|
| A number increased by $7$ | $x+7$ |
| Five more than twice a number | $2x+5$ |
| Seven less than a number | $x-7$ |
| The product of a number and $4$ | $4x$ |
| A number divided by $3$ | $\frac{x}{3}$ |
| Three times a number, decreased by $2$ | $3x-2$ |

## 2. Formula Bank

| Idea | Rule |
|---|---|
| Collecting like terms | $ax+bx=(a+b)x$ |
| Distributive property | $a(b+c)=ab+ac$ |
| Subtracting a bracket | $a-(b+c)=a-b-c$ |
| Equation: addition | $x+a=b\implies x=b-a$ |
| Equation: subtraction | $x-a=b\implies x=b+a$ |
| Equation: multiplication | $ax=b\implies x=\frac{b}{a}$, where $a\neq0$ |
| Equation: division | $\frac{x}{a}=b\implies x=ab$, where $a\neq0$ |
| Check | Substitute the solution into the original equation |

## 3. How to Solve

### Example 1: Collect like terms

**Question:** Simplify $3x+5x-2x$.

**Step 1 — All three terms are like terms.**

$$
(3+5-2)x
$$

**Step 2 — Calculate the coefficient.**

$$
\boxed{6x}
$$

### Example 2: Evaluate with a negative value

**Question:** Evaluate $3x^2-2x+1$ for $x=-2$.

**Step 1 — Substitute using brackets.**

$$
3(-2)^2-2(-2)+1
$$

**Step 2 — Evaluate the power first.**

$$
3(4)+4+1
$$

**Step 3 — Multiply, then add.**

$$
12+4+1=\boxed{17}
$$

### Example 3: Solve a two-step equation

**Question:** Solve $4x-9=15$.

**Step 1 — Add $9$ to both sides.**

$$
4x=24
$$

**Step 2 — Divide both sides by $4$.**

$$
x=\boxed{6}
$$

**Step 3 — Check.**

$$
4(6)-9=24-9=15
$$

### Example 4: Solve an equation with a fraction

**Question:** Solve $\frac{x}{5}=7$.

**Step 1 — Multiply both sides by $5$.**

$$
x=7\times5=\boxed{35}
$$

### Example 5: Translate and solve

**Question:** "Five more than twice a number is $21$." Find the number.

**Step 1 — Let the number be $x$.**

$$
2x+5=21
$$

**Step 2 — Subtract $5$.**

$$
2x=16
$$

**Step 3 — Divide by $2$.**

$$
x=\boxed{8}
$$

## 4. Watch Out!

| Mistake | Why it is wrong | Correct method |
|---|---|---|
| $x+x=x^2$ | Adding two equal terms doubles them; it does not square them. | $x+x=2x$ |
| $3x+4=7x$ | $3x$ and $4$ are unlike terms. | $3x+4$ stays as it is. |
| $2x=8\implies x=4$ written as $x=8-2$ | The operation is division, not subtraction. | Divide both sides by $2$. |
| Dropping a negative sign during transposition | Every term keeps its sign until it is moved. | Show the operation on both sides. |
| $3(x+2)=3x+2$ | The $3$ multiplies both terms inside the bracket. | $3x+6$ |
| Treating an expression as an equation | An expression has no equals sign. | Only equations are "solved". |

> **Examination tip:** After solving any equation, substitute the value back. This catches most sign and arithmetic errors before marks are lost.

## 5. Practise and Check

1. Simplify $7x+3x-4x$.
2. Simplify $5a+2b-3a+b$.
3. Evaluate $4x+1$ for $x=3$.
4. Evaluate $2x^2+3$ for $x=-2$.
5. Solve $x+9=17$.
6. Solve $5x=45$.
7. Solve $2x-7=11$.
8. Solve $\frac{x}{6}=8$.
9. Solve $3x+4=25$.
10. Write as an expression: "seven less than three times a number".
11. A number doubled and increased by $6$ gives $20$. Write an equation and solve it.
12. Check whether $x=4$ is a solution of $3x-5=7$.

**Answers**

1. $6x$
2. $2a+3b$
3. $13$
4. $11$
5. $x=8$
6. $x=9$
7. $x=9$
8. $x=48$
9. $x=7$
10. $3x-7$
11. $2x+6=20\implies 2x=14\implies x=7$
12. $3(4)-5=12-5=7$, so yes.

### Mastery check

She is ready to move on when she can:

- name the parts of an expression correctly;
- collect like terms and evaluate an expression with a negative value;
- solve one-step and two-step equations and check the answer;
- translate a short sentence into an equation.
