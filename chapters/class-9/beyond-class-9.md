## 1. Beyond Class 9

This chapter is a preview of ideas that become important in higher classes. It is a reference section, not part of the Class 9 syllabus sequence.

## 2. The Imaginary Unit

The square of every real number is non-negative. To solve equations such as $x^2=-1$, mathematicians introduce the **imaginary unit** $i$:

<div class="formula-box">

$$
\sqrt{-1}=i
$$

</div>

It follows that:

$$
i^2=-1
$$

**Example:** Simplify $\sqrt{-9}$.

$$
\sqrt{-9}=\sqrt{9\times(-1)}=\sqrt{9}\times\sqrt{-1}=3i
$$

## 3. Radian Measure

Angles can be measured in **degrees** or **radians**. A full circle is $360^\circ$, which is equal to $2\pi$ radians.

<div class="formula-box">

$$
\pi\text{ radians}=180^\circ
$$

</div>

| Radians | Degrees |
|---|---|
| $2\pi$ | $360^\circ$ |
| $\pi$ | $180^\circ$ |
| $\frac{\pi}{2}$ | $90^\circ$ |
| $\frac{\pi}{3}$ | $60^\circ$ |
| $\frac{\pi}{4}$ | $45^\circ$ |
| $\frac{\pi}{6}$ | $30^\circ$ |

### Converting between radians and degrees

To convert **degrees to radians**, multiply by $\frac{\pi}{180}$:

$$
\text{radians}=\text{degrees}\times\frac{\pi}{180}
$$

To convert **radians to degrees**, multiply by $\frac{180}{\pi}$:

$$
\text{degrees}=\text{radians}\times\frac{180}{\pi}
$$

### Worked examples

**Example 1:** Convert $120^\circ$ to radians.

$$
120^\circ\times\frac{\pi}{180}=\frac{120\pi}{180}=\frac{2\pi}{3}
$$

**Example 2:** Convert $\frac{3\pi}{4}$ radians to degrees.

$$
\frac{3\pi}{4}\times\frac{180}{\pi}=\frac{3\times180}{4}=135^\circ
$$
