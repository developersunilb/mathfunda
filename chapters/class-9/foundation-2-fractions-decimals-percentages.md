## Foundation 2: Fractions, Decimals and Percentages

Fractions, decimals and percentages are three ways of writing the same kind of quantity. If the diagnostic showed mistakes in questions 3, 4, 5, 7, 14 or 15, this lesson repairs those skills.

## 1. Remember the Basics

### What a fraction means

A fraction $\frac{a}{b}$ means $a$ equal parts out of $b$ equal parts of a whole. The number below the line is the **denominator**; it tells how many equal parts the whole is divided into. The number above the line is the **numerator**; it tells how many parts are taken.

$$
\frac{3}{4}\quad\text{means 3 parts out of 4 equal parts.}
$$

The denominator cannot be zero, because a whole cannot be divided into zero equal parts.

### Types of fractions

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Proper fraction | Numerator is smaller than the denominator. | $\frac{3}{5}$ |
| Improper fraction | Numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator. | $\frac{7}{4}$ |
| Mixed number | A whole number and a proper fraction. | $2\frac{3}{4}$ |

To change a mixed number to an improper fraction:

$$
2\frac{3}{4}=\frac{2\times4+3}{4}=\frac{11}{4}
$$

### Equivalent fractions and simplest form

Multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the **same non-zero number** gives an equivalent fraction:

$$
\frac{1}{2}=\frac{2}{4}=\frac{3}{6}
$$

To simplify, divide both parts by their **highest common factor (HCF)**.

### Comparing fractions

For $\frac{a}{b}$ and $\frac{c}{d}$ with positive denominators, cross-multiply:

$$
\frac{a}{b}>\frac{c}{d}\quad\text{when}\quad ad>bc
$$

### Decimals

Each place after the decimal point has a value:

| Place | Value |
|---|---|
| Tenths | $\frac{1}{10}=0.1$ |
| Hundredths | $\frac{1}{100}=0.01$ |
| Thousandths | $\frac{1}{1000}=0.001$ |

So $0.375$ means $3$ tenths, $7$ hundredths and $5$ thousandths.

### Percentages

**Per cent** means "per hundred". Therefore:

$$
15\%=\frac{15}{100}=0.15
$$

A percentage is a fraction with denominator $100$. It is not a separate kind of number.

### Factors, multiples, HCF and LCM

- A **factor** divides a number exactly.
- A **multiple** is the result of multiplying a number by a whole number.
- The **HCF** is the largest common factor.
- The **LCM** is the smallest common multiple.

**Prime factorisation** writes a number using only primes:

$$
18=2\times3^2,\qquad 24=2^3\times3
$$

For the HCF, take the **lowest** power of each common prime. For the LCM, take the **highest** power of every prime that appears.

$$
\text{HCF}(18,24)=2\times3=6
$$

$$
\text{LCM}(18,24)=2^3\times3^2=72
$$

> **Useful check for two positive integers:** $\text{HCF}\times\text{LCM}=\text{product of the two numbers}$.

## 2. Formula Bank

| Skill | Rule |
|---|---|
| Equivalent fractions | $\frac{a}{b}=\frac{ak}{bk}$, where $k\neq0$ |
| Simplifying | Divide numerator and denominator by their HCF |
| Comparing | $\frac{a}{b}>\frac{c}{d}$ when $ad>bc$ (positive denominators) |
| Adding or subtracting | Make denominators equal first: $\frac{a}{b}\pm\frac{c}{d}=\frac{ad\pm bc}{bd}$ |
| Multiplying | $\frac{a}{b}\times\frac{c}{d}=\frac{ac}{bd}$ |
| Dividing | $\frac{a}{b}\div\frac{c}{d}=\frac{a}{b}\times\frac{d}{c}$, where $c\neq0$ |
| Mixed to improper | $m\frac{a}{b}=\frac{mb+a}{b}$ |
| Fraction to decimal | Divide the numerator by the denominator |
| Decimal to fraction | Write over $10$, $100$, $1000$, then simplify |
| Decimal to percentage | Multiply by $100$ |
| Percentage to decimal | Divide by $100$ |
| Percentage of a quantity | $\frac{p}{100}\times\text{quantity}$ |
| Percentage increase or decrease | $\frac{\text{change}}{\text{original}}\times100\%$ |
| HCF and LCM by primes | HCF: lowest common powers; LCM: highest powers |
| Square root by primes | Group the prime factors into identical pairs and take one from each pair |

## 3. How to Solve

### Example 1: Simplify a fraction

**Question:** Write $\frac{18}{24}$ in simplest form.

**Step 1 — Find the HCF of $18$ and $24$.** The HCF is $6$.

**Step 2 — Divide both parts by $6$.**

$$
\frac{18\div6}{24\div6}=\boxed{\frac{3}{4}}
$$

### Example 2: Compare two fractions

**Question:** Which is greater, $\frac{3}{4}$ or $\frac{5}{7}$?

**Step 1 — Cross-multiply.**

$$
3\times7=21,\qquad 5\times4=20
$$

**Step 2 — Compare the products.** Since $21>20$,

$$
\boxed{\frac{3}{4}>\frac{5}{7}}
$$

### Example 3: Add fractions

**Question:** Find $\frac{2}{3}+\frac{3}{4}$.

**Step 1 — Find the LCM of the denominators.** The LCM of $3$ and $4$ is $12$.

**Step 2 — Make equivalent fractions.**

$$
\frac{2}{3}=\frac{8}{12},\qquad \frac{3}{4}=\frac{9}{12}
$$

**Step 3 — Add the numerators; keep the denominator.**

$$
\frac{8}{12}+\frac{9}{12}=\frac{17}{12}=\boxed{1\frac{5}{12}}
$$

<div class="formula-box">

**Why the denominators are not added:** The denominator names the size of the parts. Adding thirds to quarters does not create sevenths. We first rewrite both amounts in the same-sized parts (twelfths), then add.

</div>

### Example 4: Subtract mixed numbers

**Question:** Find $3\frac{1}{2}-1\frac{3}{4}$.

**Step 1 — Change both to improper fractions.**

$$
3\frac{1}{2}=\frac{7}{2},\qquad 1\frac{3}{4}=\frac{7}{4}
$$

**Step 2 — Use the LCM $4$.**

$$
\frac{7}{2}=\frac{14}{4}
$$

**Step 3 — Subtract.**

$$
\frac{14}{4}-\frac{7}{4}=\frac{7}{4}=\boxed{1\frac{3}{4}}
$$

### Example 5: Multiply fractions

**Question:** Find $2\frac{1}{2}\times1\frac{1}{3}$.

**Step 1 — Change to improper fractions.**

$$
\frac{5}{2}\times\frac{4}{3}
$$

**Step 2 — Multiply numerators and denominators.**

$$
\frac{5\times4}{2\times3}=\frac{20}{6}
$$

**Step 3 — Simplify.**

$$
\frac{10}{3}=\boxed{3\frac{1}{3}}
$$

### Example 6: Divide fractions

**Question:** Find $\frac{5}{6}\div\frac{2}{3}$.

**Step 1 — Multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction.**

$$
\frac{5}{6}\times\frac{3}{2}
$$

**Step 2 — Multiply.**

$$
\frac{15}{12}
$$

**Step 3 — Simplify by dividing by $3$.**

$$
\boxed{\frac{5}{4}=1\frac{1}{4}}
$$

> **Why this works:** Dividing by $\frac{2}{3}$ asks "how many thirds-of-a-half fit?" Multiplying by $\frac{3}{2}$ is the same operation because $\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{3}{2}=1$.

### Example 7: Multiply decimals

**Question:** Calculate $3.75\times0.4$.

**Step 1 — Multiply as whole numbers.**

$$
375\times4=1500
$$

**Step 2 — Count the decimal places.** There are $2$ places in $3.75$ and $1$ place in $0.4$, so $3$ places in total.

**Step 3 — Place the decimal point.**

$$
\boxed{1.500=1.5}
$$

### Example 8: Decimal to fraction

**Question:** Write $0.375$ as a fraction in simplest form.

**Step 1 — Write over $1000$ because there are three decimal places.**

$$
0.375=\frac{375}{1000}
$$

**Step 2 — Divide by the HCF $125$.**

$$
\frac{375\div125}{1000\div125}=\boxed{\frac{3}{8}}
$$

### Example 9: Percentage of a quantity

**Question:** Find $15\%$ of $240$.

**Step 1 — Write the percentage as a fraction or decimal.**

$$
15\%=\frac{15}{100}=0.15
$$

**Step 2 — Multiply.**

$$
0.15\times240=\boxed{36}
$$

### Example 10: Percentage increase

**Question:** A price rises from ₹$400$ to ₹$460$. Find the percentage increase.

**Step 1 — Find the change.**

$$
460-400=60
$$

**Step 2 — Divide by the original price.**

$$
\frac{60}{400}=\frac{3}{20}=0.15
$$

**Step 3 — Convert to a percentage.**

$$
0.15\times100\%=\boxed{15\%}
$$

### Example 11: Square root by prime factorisation

**Question:** Find $\sqrt{324}$.

**Step 1 — Write the prime factorisation.**

$$
324=2\times2\times3\times3\times3\times3
$$

**Step 2 — Group into identical pairs.**

$$
324=(2\times2)\times(3\times3)\times(3\times3)
$$

**Step 3 — Take one factor from each pair.**

$$
\sqrt{324}=2\times3\times3=\boxed{18}
$$

## 4. Watch Out!

| Mistake | Why it is wrong | Correct method |
|---|---|---|
| $\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}=\frac{2}{5}$ | Denominators are not added. | $\frac{3}{6}+\frac{2}{6}=\frac{5}{6}$ |
| Dividing by flipping the **first** fraction | The divisor is the fraction being divided by. | Flip the **second** fraction only. |
| Multiplying mixed numbers directly | The whole-number part is not multiplied correctly. | Change to improper fractions first. |
| $0.4\times0.3=1.2$ | The decimal places were not counted. | $0.4\times0.3=0.12$ |
| Finding $15\%$ of a value by dividing by $15$ | Percent means per hundred. | Multiply by $\frac{15}{100}=0.15$. |
| Using the new price as the base for a percentage increase | The percentage is measured from the original value. | Divide the change by the **original** value. |
| Writing $0.5\%$ as $0.5$ | Percent is per hundred. | $0.5\%=0.005$ |
| Assuming every fraction has a terminating decimal | Only denominators of the form $2^m5^n$ after simplification terminate. | $\frac{1}{3}=0.333\ldots$ repeats forever. |

> **Examination tip:** In a multi-step fraction question, simplify before multiplying. Smaller numbers reduce arithmetic errors and save time.

## 5. Practise and Check

Attempt these without looking at the answers.

1. Simplify $\frac{24}{36}$.
2. Compare $\frac{5}{8}$ and $\frac{7}{12}$.
3. Find $\frac{3}{5}+\frac{1}{4}$.
4. Find $\frac{7}{8}-\frac{1}{3}$.
5. Find $\frac{2}{5}\times\frac{15}{8}$.
6. Find $\frac{3}{4}\div\frac{9}{8}$.
7. Change $4\frac{2}{5}$ to an improper fraction.
8. Calculate $2.4\times0.25$.
9. Write $0.84$ as a fraction in simplest form.
10. Find $12\%$ of $450$.
11. A class has $40$ students and $24$ are girls. What percentage are girls?
12. Find the HCF and LCM of $12$ and $30$ by prime factorisation.
13. Find $\sqrt{576}$ by prime factorisation.
14. A shirt costs ₹$800$. Its price is reduced by $20\%$. Find the new price.

**Answers**

1. $\frac{2}{3}$
2. $\frac{5}{8}>\frac{7}{12}$, since $5\times12=60>56=7\times8$.
3. $\frac{17}{20}$
4. $\frac{13}{24}$
5. $\frac{3}{4}$
6. $\frac{2}{3}$
7. $\frac{22}{5}$
8. $0.6$
9. $\frac{21}{25}$
10. $54$
11. $60\%$
12. $12=2^2\times3$ and $30=2\times3\times5$, so HCF $=6$ and LCM $=60$.
13. $576=2^6\times3^2$, so $\sqrt{576}=2^3\times3=24$.
14. Reduction $=0.20\times800=160$, so the new price is ₹$640$.

### Mastery check

She is ready to move on when she can:

- add and subtract fractions by finding a common denominator;
- multiply and divide fractions, including mixed numbers;
- convert between fractions, decimals and percentages;
- find a percentage of a quantity and a percentage change;
- use prime factorisation for HCF, LCM and square roots.

If one of these still feels uncertain, practise three more examples of that type before continuing.
