## Foundation 3: Ratio and Proportion

Ratios compare quantities. They appear in maps, recipes, mixtures, speed, scale drawings, sharing money and similar triangles. If the diagnostic showed a mistake in the ratio question, repair it here.

## 1. Remember the Basics

### What a ratio means

A ratio compares two quantities of the **same kind** using division:

$$
a:b=\frac{a}{b}\qquad (b\neq0)
$$

If a bag has $6$ red and $9$ blue counters, the ratio of red to blue is

$$
6:9=2:3
$$

The order matters: red to blue is $2:3$, but blue to red is $3:2$.

### Simplifying a ratio

Divide every part by the same non-zero number, just as you simplify a fraction.

$$
18:24=3:4
$$

Ratios have no units when both quantities are measured in the same unit. If the units differ, convert to the same unit first.

### Sharing in a ratio

To divide a quantity in the ratio $a:b$:

1. Add the ratio parts: $a+b$.
2. Divide the quantity by this total to find one part.
3. Multiply by each ratio number.

**Example:** Divide ₹$360$ in the ratio $2:3$.

$$
2+3=5\text{ parts}
$$

$$
1\text{ part}=\frac{360}{5}=72
$$

$$
2\text{ parts}=144,\qquad 3\text{ parts}=216
$$

### Proportion

A proportion says two ratios are equal:

$$
a:b=c:d\quad\iff\quad ad=bc
$$

The cross-products are equal. This is called the **cross-multiplication rule**.

### Direct proportion

Two quantities are in **direct proportion** when one increases as the other increases, at the same rate:

$$
\frac{y}{x}=k\quad\text{or}\quad y=kx
$$

Examples: the cost of pens and the number of pens; distance travelled at constant speed and time.

### Inverse proportion

Two quantities are in **inverse proportion** when one increases as the other decreases, so their product stays fixed:

$$
xy=k
$$

Example: the number of workers and the time taken to finish the same job.

### The unitary method

Find the value of **one** unit first, then multiply.

**Example:** If $5$ pens cost ₹$60$, then one pen costs ₹$12$, so $8$ pens cost ₹$96$.

## 2. Formula Bank

| Idea | Rule |
|---|---|
| Ratio | $a:b=\frac{a}{b}$, where $b\neq0$ |
| Simplifying | Divide all parts by their HCF |
| Proportion | $a:b=c:d\iff ad=bc$ |
| Direct proportion | $\frac{y_1}{x_1}=\frac{y_2}{x_2}$ |
| Inverse proportion | $x_1y_1=x_2y_2$ |
| Sharing in ratio | One part $=\frac{\text{total}}{\text{sum of ratio parts}}$ |
| Unitary method | Value of one unit $=\frac{\text{total value}}{\text{number of units}}$ |
| Scale | $\text{scale}=\frac{\text{drawing length}}{\text{actual length}}$ |

## 3. How to Solve

### Example 1: Simplify a ratio with units

**Question:** Write $45\text{ minutes}:2\text{ hours}$ in simplest form.

**Step 1 — Convert to the same unit.** $2\text{ hours}=120\text{ minutes}$.

**Step 2 — Write the ratio.**

$$
45:120
$$

**Step 3 — Divide both parts by the HCF $15$.**

$$
\boxed{3:8}
$$

### Example 2: Share a quantity

**Question:** Divide ₹$720$ in the ratio $4:5$.

**Step 1 — Add the parts.**

$$
4+5=9
$$

**Step 2 — Find one part.**

$$
720\div9=80
$$

**Step 3 — Multiply each share.**

$$
4\times80=320,\qquad 5\times80=400
$$

$$
\boxed{₹320\text{ and }₹400}
$$

### Example 3: Direct proportion

**Question:** If $5$ pens cost ₹$60$, what do $8$ pens cost?

**Step 1 — Find the cost of one pen.**

$$
60\div5=12
$$

**Step 2 — Multiply by $8$.**

$$
12\times8=\boxed{₹96}
$$

### Example 4: Inverse proportion

**Question:** Six workers finish a job in $12$ days. How long would $9$ workers take, working at the same rate?

**Step 1 — More workers means less time, so this is inverse proportion.**

$$
x_1y_1=x_2y_2
$$

**Step 2 — Substitute.**

$$
6\times12=9\times t
$$

**Step 3 — Solve.**

$$
t=\frac{72}{9}=\boxed{8\text{ days}}
$$

### Example 5: Cross-multiplication

**Question:** Are the ratios $3:4$ and $15:20$ in proportion?

**Step 1 — Compare the cross-products.**

$$
3\times20=60,\qquad 4\times15=60
$$

**Step 2 — The cross-products are equal, so the ratios are in proportion.**

$$
\boxed{3:4=15:20}
$$

## 4. Watch Out!

| Mistake | Why it is wrong | Correct method |
|---|---|---|
| Comparing $45\text{ min}:2\text{ h}$ as $45:2$ | The units differ. | Convert: $45:120=3:8$. |
| Adding ratio numbers as if they were quantities | $2:3$ does not mean ₹$2$ and ₹$3$. | Use the total and the sum of parts. |
| Using direct proportion for a work problem | More workers reduce the time. | Use inverse proportion. |
| Writing $a:b=b:a$ | Order matters. | Keep the order stated in the question. |
| Dividing a total by the number of ratio terms only | The parts are not equal unless the ratio says so. | Divide by the **sum** of the ratio parts. |

> **Examination tip:** Before solving a proportion problem, ask: "If one quantity doubles, does the other double or halve?" Double means direct; halve means inverse.

## 5. Practise and Check

1. Simplify $24:36$.
2. Write $40\text{ minutes}:1\text{ hour}$ in simplest form.
3. Divide ₹$540$ in the ratio $2:7$.
4. If $4$ notebooks cost ₹$100$, find the cost of $7$ notebooks.
5. Are $5:8$ and $15:24$ in proportion?
6. Eight machines take $15$ days to complete an order. How long would $12$ machines take at the same rate?
7. A recipe uses flour and sugar in the ratio $5:2$. If it uses $350\text{ g}$ of flour, how much sugar is needed?
8. A map uses a scale of $1\text{ cm}:5\text{ km}$. What real distance does $3.5\text{ cm}$ on the map represent?
9. The ratio of boys to girls in a class is $3:4$. If there are $28$ girls, how many boys are there?
10. A car travels $180\text{ km}$ in $3$ hours. At the same speed, how far does it travel in $5$ hours?

**Answers**

1. $2:3$
2. $2:3$
3. $120$ and $420$
4. ₹$175$
5. Yes: $5\times24=120=8\times15$.
6. $10$ days (inverse proportion: $8\times15=12\times10$).
7. $140\text{ g}$
8. $17.5\text{ km}$
9. $21$ boys
10. $300\text{ km}$

### Mastery check

She is ready to move on when she can:

- simplify a ratio and convert units before comparing;
- share a quantity in a given ratio;
- decide whether a situation is direct or inverse proportion;
- use the unitary method and cross-multiplication confidently.
