# Linear Inequalities: Worked Examples

Worked examples for **Linear Inequalities**: solving and graphing, including the sign-flip rule.

## Example 1: Solve a simple inequality

**Question:** Solve $x+5>12$.

**Method:** Subtract 5 from both sides. The inequality sign stays the same because we only added/subtracted.

$$
x>12-5=7
$$

$$
\boxed{x>7}
$$

## Example 2: Divide by a negative (sign flips)

**Question:** Solve $-2x<10$.

**Method:** Divide by $-2$. When multiplying or dividing by a negative number, the inequality sign flips.

$$
x>\frac{10}{-2}=-5
$$

$$
\boxed{x>-5}
$$

## Example 3: Two-step inequality

**Question:** Solve $3x-4<8$.

**Method:** Add 4, then divide by 3 (positive, so no flip).

**Step 1 — Add 4.**

$$
3x<12
$$

**Step 2 — Divide by 3.**

$$
x<4
$$

$$
\boxed{x<4}
$$

## Example 4: Graph an inequality on a number line

**Question:** Show $x>3$ on a number line.

**Method:** Draw an open circle at 3 (since 3 is not included) and shade to the right.

## Example 5: Inequality from a word problem

**Question:** A student needs at least 40 marks to pass. If she already has 28, how many more $m$ does she need?

**Method:** "At least" means $\ge$. Total $=28+m\ge40$.

**Step 1 — Write inequality.**

$$
28+m\ge40
$$

**Step 2 — Solve.**

$$
m\ge40-28=12
$$

$$
\boxed{m\ge12}
$$
