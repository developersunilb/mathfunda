# Chapter 3: The World of Numbers

## 3.1 History of counting

Numbers evolved from tally marks to place value. Civilisations developed symbols and positional notation.

## 3.2 Zero

Zero as placeholder and number (Brahmagupta). It represents absence and enables place value.

## 3.3 Integers

Integers include negatives, zero, positives. Brahmagupta's rules for addition and subtraction.

## 3.4 Rational and irrational

Rational numbers are p/q with q≠0. Irrational like √2 cannot be expressed as p/q.

**Proof that √2 is irrational:** Assume √2 = p/q in lowest terms. Then 2q² = p², so p² even ⇒ p even. Let p=2k. Then 2q² = 4k² ⇒ q² = 2k², so q even. Contradiction (p,q both even). Hence √2 irrational.

## 3.5 Real decimals

Terminating decimals (finite), repeating decimals (rational), non-terminating non-repeating (irrational). π and √2 are irrational decimals.
