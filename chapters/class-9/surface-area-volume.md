# Chapter 14: Math of Space — Surface Area and Volume

## 14.1 Why space matters

Every object you hold has three dimensions: length, breadth and height. The amount of material needed to make it is its **surface area**; the space it occupies is its **volume**. These two ideas govern packaging, water tanks, domes, ice-cream cones and footballs.

## 14.2 Cuboid and cube

For a cuboid with length $l$, breadth $b$, height $h$:

- Total surface area: $2(lb + bh + hl)$
- Lateral (curved) surface area: $2h(l+b)$
- Volume: $l \times b \times h$

For a cube of side $a$: TSA $= 6a^2$, LSA $= 4a^2$, Volume $= a^3$.

**Example.** A cuboid of dimensions $4\,\text{cm}\times 3\,\text{cm}\times 2\,\text{cm}$:
- TSA $= 2(12+6+8)=52\,\text{cm}^2$
- Volume $= 4\times3\times2=24\,\text{cm}^3$.

## 14.3 Cylinder

For a right circular cylinder of radius $r$ and height $h$:

- TSA $= 2\pi r(r+h)$
- CSA (curved) $= 2\pi r h$
- Volume $= \pi r^2 h$

## 14.4 Cone

For a right circular cone of radius $r$, height $h$, slant height $l=\sqrt{r^2+h^2}$:

- CSA $= \pi r l$
- TSA $= \pi r(l+r)$
- Volume $= \frac13 \pi r^2 h$

## 14.5 Sphere and hemisphere

Sphere of radius $r$: surface area $= 4\pi r^2$, volume $= \frac43\pi r^3$.

Hemisphere: CSA $= 2\pi r^2$, TSA $= 3\pi r^2$, volume $= \frac23\pi r^3$.

## 14.6 Pyramid

Right pyramid with base area $B$ and height $h$: volume $= \frac13 B h$. (Lateral area depends on slant heights of faces.)

## 14.7 Composite solids

Break the solid into standard parts, compute each part, then add (for surface area, subtract hidden faces) or sum volumes.

**Example.** A cylinder of radius $3$ cm and height $5$ cm with a hemisphere of radius $3$ cm on top:
- Volume $= \pi(3)^2(5) + \frac23\pi(3)^3 = 45\pi + 18\pi = 63\pi\,\text{cm}^3$.

## 14.8 Unit conversion

- $1\,\text{m}^3 = 1000\,\text{L}$; $1\,\text{L}=1000\,\text{cm}^3$.
- Surface area units are squared; volume units are cubed.

## Formula reference

| Formula | Meaning |
|---|---|
| TSA cuboid = 2(lb+bh+hl) | Total surface area |
| Volume cuboid = l×b×h | Volume |
| Volume cylinder = πr²h | Volume |
| CSA cone = πrl | Curved surface area |
