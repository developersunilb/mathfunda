## 1. Linear Inequalities

> **Enrichment note:** Linear inequalities in one variable are useful and commonly taught, but they are not part of the core Class 9 sequence in this guide. Study them when the school prescribes them or when the foundations are secure.

An inequality compares two expressions instead of saying they are equal.

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $<$ | less than |
| $>$ | greater than |
| $\le$ | less than or equal to |
| $\ge$ | greater than or equal to |

A linear inequality in one variable has the general form

$$
ax+b<c \quad\text{or}\quad ax+b\ge c
$$

where $a\neq 0$.

## 2. Rules for Solving Inequalities

- Adding or subtracting the same number on both sides does not change the inequality.
- Multiplying or dividing both sides by a **positive** number does not change the inequality.
- Multiplying or dividing both sides by a **negative** number **reverses** the inequality sign.

> **Remember:** The inequality sign flips only when you multiply or divide by a negative number.

## 3. Boxed Notes and Worked Examples

<div class="formula-box">

### Example 1: Flipping the Inequality Sign

**Problem:** Solve $-3x \le 12$.

Divide both sides by $-3$. Because we are dividing by a negative number, the inequality sign flips:

$$
\frac{-3x}{-3}\ge\frac{12}{-3}
$$

$$
x\ge -4
$$

**Solution:** $\boxed{x\ge -4}$

</div>

<div class="formula-box">

### Example 2: Solving a Fractional Inequality

**Problem:** Solve $\frac{4x+9}{11}>5$.

**Step 1:** Multiply both sides by $11$:

$$
4x+9>55
$$

**Step 2:** Subtract $9$ from both sides:

$$
4x>46
$$

**Step 3:** Divide both sides by $4$:

$$
x>\frac{46}{4}=11\frac{1}{2}
$$

**Solution:** $\boxed{x>11\frac{1}{2}}$

</div>
