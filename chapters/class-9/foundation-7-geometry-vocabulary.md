## Foundation 7: Geometry Vocabulary

Geometry questions are lost when the diagram is misread or a property is named incorrectly. This lesson repairs the basic vocabulary, angle facts and shape names needed for circles, quadrilaterals and proofs.

## 1. Remember the Basics

### Points, lines and planes

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Point | An exact position; named with a capital letter, such as $A$. |
| Line | A straight path extending without end in both directions. |
| Line segment | A part of a line with two endpoints. |
| Ray | A part of a line with one endpoint that continues in one direction. |
| Plane | A flat surface extending without end. |

### Angles

An angle is formed by two rays with a common endpoint called the **vertex**. Angles are measured in **degrees** ($^\circ$).

| Type | Size |
|---|---|
| Acute | Between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ |
| Right | Exactly $90^\circ$ |
| Obtuse | Between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$ |
| Straight | Exactly $180^\circ$ |
| Reflex | Between $180^\circ$ and $360^\circ$ |

### Special angle pairs

| Pair | Relationship |
|---|---|
| Complementary angles | Add to $90^\circ$ |
| Supplementary angles | Add to $180^\circ$ |
| Angles on a straight line | Add to $180^\circ$ |
| Angles around a point | Add to $360^\circ$ |
| Vertically opposite angles | Equal |

When two straight lines cross, the angles opposite each other are **vertically opposite** and equal.

### Parallel lines and transversals

A **transversal** is a line that crosses two or more lines. When the lines are parallel:

| Pair | Relationship |
|---|---|
| Corresponding angles | Equal |
| Alternate interior angles | Equal |
| Co-interior (allied) angles | Add to $180^\circ$ |

### Triangles

| Classified by sides | Property |
|---|---|
| Equilateral | All three sides equal; all angles $60^\circ$ |
| Isosceles | Two sides equal; base angles equal |
| Scalene | All sides different |

| Classified by angles | Property |
|---|---|
| Acute-angled | All angles acute |
| Right-angled | One angle is $90^\circ$ |
| Obtuse-angled | One angle is obtuse |

<div class="formula-box">

**Angle sum of a triangle:** The three interior angles add to $180^\circ$.

$$
\angle A+\angle B+\angle C=180^\circ
$$

**Exterior angle:** An exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles.

</div>

### Quadrilaterals

| Shape | Key property |
|---|---|
| Square | Four equal sides and four right angles |
| Rectangle | Opposite sides equal and four right angles |
| Parallelogram | Both pairs of opposite sides parallel |
| Rhombus | Four equal sides |
| Trapezium | Exactly one pair of parallel sides |
| Kite | Two pairs of adjacent equal sides |

The angle sum of any quadrilateral is $360^\circ$.

> **Careful with "all" statements:** Every square is a rectangle, but not every rectangle is a square. Every square is a rhombus, but not every rhombus is a square.

### Circle vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Centre | The fixed middle point |
| Radius | Distance from the centre to any point on the circle |
| Diameter | A chord passing through the centre; $d=2r$ |
| Chord | A segment joining two points on the circle |
| Arc | A part of the circle |
| Sector | The region between two radii and an arc |
| Segment | The region between a chord and its arc |
| Circumference | The distance around the circle |

## 2. Formula Bank

| Fact | Rule |
|---|---|
| Angles on a straight line | Add to $180^\circ$ |
| Angles around a point | Add to $360^\circ$ |
| Complementary | Add to $90^\circ$ |
| Supplementary | Add to $180^\circ$ |
| Vertically opposite angles | Equal |
| Triangle angle sum | $180^\circ$ |
| Quadrilateral angle sum | $360^\circ$ |
| Exterior angle of a triangle | Sum of the two opposite interior angles |
| Isosceles triangle | Base angles are equal |
| Equilateral triangle | Each angle is $60^\circ$ |
| Circle | $d=2r$ |

## 3. How to Solve

### Example 1: Third angle of a triangle

**Question:** Two angles of a triangle are $48^\circ$ and $67^\circ$. Find the third angle.

**Step 1 — Use the angle sum.**

$$
48^\circ+67^\circ+x=180^\circ
$$

**Step 2 — Add the known angles.**

$$
115^\circ+x=180^\circ
$$

**Step 3 — Subtract.**

$$
x=180^\circ-115^\circ=\boxed{65^\circ}
$$

### Example 2: Angles on a straight line

**Question:** Two angles on a straight line are $3x$ and $2x$. Find $x$.

**Step 1 — A straight angle is $180^\circ$.**

$$
3x+2x=180^\circ
$$

**Step 2 — Collect like terms and solve.**

$$
5x=180^\circ\implies x=\boxed{36^\circ}
$$

### Example 3: Vertically opposite angles

**Question:** Two vertically opposite angles are $4x+10^\circ$ and $6x-20^\circ$. Find $x$.

**Step 1 — Vertically opposite angles are equal.**

$$
4x+10=6x-20
$$

**Step 2 — Solve.**

$$
30=2x\implies x=\boxed{15}
$$

### Example 4: Isosceles triangle

**Question:** The vertex angle of an isosceles triangle is $40^\circ$. Find each base angle.

**Step 1 — The two base angles are equal. Let each be $x$.**

$$
40^\circ+x+x=180^\circ
$$

**Step 2 — Solve.**

$$
2x=140^\circ\implies x=\boxed{70^\circ}
$$

### Example 5: Exterior angle

**Question:** An exterior angle of a triangle is $115^\circ$. One opposite interior angle is $50^\circ$. Find the other.

**Step 1 — The exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles.**

$$
115^\circ=50^\circ+y
$$

**Step 2 — Subtract.**

$$
y=\boxed{65^\circ}
$$

## 4. Watch Out!

| Mistake | Why it is wrong | Correct method |
|---|---|---|
| Assuming a shape is a square because it looks square | A diagram is not a proof. | Use the given marks and properties. |
| Using $d=r$ for a circle | The diameter is twice the radius. | $d=2r$ |
| Saying all rectangles are squares | A rectangle need not have equal sides. | All squares are rectangles; the converse is false. |
| Using the exterior angle as an interior angle | They lie on different sides of a side of the triangle. | The two angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$. |
| Writing "the angle is $50$" without the degree symbol | Degrees are the unit of angle measure. | Write $50^\circ$. |

> **Examination tip:** In a geometry answer, name the property used, for example: "Angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$." A correct number without a reason may lose method marks.

## 5. Practise and Check

1. Classify an angle of $135^\circ$.
2. Find the complement of $37^\circ$.
3. Find the supplement of $112^\circ$.
4. Two angles on a straight line are $x$ and $4x$. Find $x$.
5. Two vertically opposite angles are $5x$ and $80^\circ$. Find $x$.
6. Two angles of a triangle are $35^\circ$ and $85^\circ$. Find the third angle.
7. Each base angle of an isosceles triangle is $55^\circ$. Find the vertex angle.
8. An exterior angle is $130^\circ$ and one opposite interior angle is $60^\circ$. Find the other opposite interior angle.
9. Name the quadrilateral with four equal sides but no requirement of right angles.
10. The radius of a circle is $9\text{ cm}$. Find its diameter.

**Answers**

1. Obtuse
2. $53^\circ$
3. $68^\circ$
4. $x=36^\circ$
5. $x=16$
6. $60^\circ$
7. $70^\circ$
8. $70^\circ$
9. Rhombus
10. $18\text{ cm}$

### Mastery check

She is ready to move on when she can:

- label points, lines, rays and angles correctly on a diagram;
- state and use the angle facts for straight lines, points and triangles;
- recognise the main quadrilaterals and circle parts;
- distinguish a property that is given from one that merely looks true.
