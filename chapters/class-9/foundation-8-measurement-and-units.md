## Foundation 8: Measurement and Units

Mensuration marks are easy to lose through unit mistakes. This lesson repairs metric conversions, the meaning of square and cubic units, and the basic measurement formulas before the fuller mensuration chapters.

## 1. Remember the Basics

### Metric conversions

| Length | Mass | Capacity |
|---|---|---|
| $1\text{ km}=1000\text{ m}$ | $1\text{ kg}=1000\text{ g}$ | $1\text{ L}=1000\text{ mL}$ |
| $1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}$ | $1\text{ g}=1000\text{ mg}$ | $1\text{ L}=1000\text{ cm}^3$ |
| $1\text{ cm}=10\text{ mm}$ | $1\text{ tonne}=1000\text{ kg}$ | $1\text{ m}^3=1000\text{ L}$ |

### One rule for every conversion

Write the metric ladder once and most conversion questions become a decimal shift:

| Prefix | kilo- | hecto- | deca- | metre / gram / litre | deci- | centi- | milli- |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Value | $1000$ | $100$ | $10$ | $1$ | $0.1$ | $0.01$ | $0.001$ |

- **Going down** the ladder (bigger unit $\to$ smaller unit), **multiply**.
- **Going up** the ladder (smaller unit $\to$ bigger unit), **divide**.
- Each step is one power of ten. Kilo to the base unit is three places, centi is two,
  milli is three.

$$
2.6\text{ km}=2.6\times1000=2600\text{ m}
$$

$$
350\text{ m}=350\div1000=0.35\text{ km}
$$

$$
45\text{ cm}=45\div100=0.45\text{ m}
$$

$$
7.2\text{ m}=7.2\times100=720\text{ cm}
$$

### Converting in either direction

| Conversion | Operation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| $\text{km}\to\text{m}$ | Multiply by $1000$ | $3\text{ km}=3000\text{ m}$ |
| $\text{m}\to\text{km}$ | Divide by $1000$ | $4500\text{ m}=4.5\text{ km}$ |
| $\text{m}\to\text{cm}$ | Multiply by $100$ | $2.4\text{ m}=240\text{ cm}$ |
| $\text{cm}\to\text{m}$ | Divide by $100$ | $85\text{ cm}=0.85\text{ m}$ |
| $\text{cm}\to\text{mm}$ | Multiply by $10$ | $6\text{ cm}=60\text{ mm}$ |
| $\text{mm}\to\text{cm}$ | Divide by $10$ | $42\text{ mm}=4.2\text{ cm}$ |
| $\text{km}\to\text{cm}$ | Multiply by $100{,}000$ | $1.5\text{ km}=150{,}000\text{ cm}$ |
| $\text{cm}\to\text{km}$ | Divide by $100{,}000$ | $250{,}000\text{ cm}=2.5\text{ km}$ |
| $\text{kg}\to\text{g}$ | Multiply by $1000$ | $2.4\text{ kg}=2400\text{ g}$ |
| $\text{g}\to\text{kg}$ | Divide by $1000$ | $750\text{ g}=0.75\text{ kg}$ |
| $\text{L}\to\text{mL}$ | Multiply by $1000$ | $1.5\text{ L}=1500\text{ mL}$ |
| $\text{mL}\to\text{L}$ | Divide by $1000$ | $250\text{ mL}=0.25\text{ L}$ |

### Shortcuts that save time

- **Learn three anchors, not a dozen rules:** $1\text{ km}=1000\text{ m}$,
  $1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}$, $1\text{ cm}=10\text{ mm}$. Every other length
  conversion is built from them.
- **Move the decimal point instead of multiplying.** $\times1000$ shifts it three
  places right; $\div1000$ shifts it three places left. For example
  $4.08\text{ m}=408\text{ cm}$.
- **Useful long facts:** $1\text{ km}=100{,}000\text{ cm}$ (five zeros) and
  $1\text{ m}=1000\text{ mm}$.
- **Area squares the factor; volume cubes it.** A length factor of $100$ becomes
  $100^2=10{,}000$ for area and $100^3=1{,}000{,}000$ for volume.
- **Sanity check:** converting to a smaller unit must give a **bigger** number.
  If $3\text{ m}$ becomes $0.03\text{ cm}$, the operation went the wrong way.

### Converting speeds

Speed is where units most often go wrong. Learn the two fractions:

$$
1\text{ km/h}=\frac{1000\text{ m}}{3600\text{ s}}=\frac{5}{18}\text{ m/s}
$$

$$
1\text{ m/s}=\frac{18}{5}\text{ km/h}
$$

So multiply by $\frac{5}{18}$ to change $\text{km/h}$ into $\text{m/s}$, and by
$\frac{18}{5}$ for the reverse. Two anchors cover most questions:

$$
36\text{ km/h}=10\text{ m/s},\qquad 72\text{ km/h}=20\text{ m/s}
$$

For time, remember the common fractions of an hour:
$15\text{ min}=0.25\text{ h}$, $20\text{ min}=\frac{1}{3}\text{ h}$,
$30\text{ min}=0.5\text{ h}$, $45\text{ min}=0.75\text{ h}$.

### Time conversions

$$
1\text{ hour}=60\text{ minutes},\qquad 1\text{ minute}=60\text{ seconds}
$$

$$
1\text{ day}=24\text{ hours}
$$

### Perimeter, area and volume

- **Perimeter** is the distance around a shape. It is measured in ordinary units: $\text{cm}$, $\text{m}$, $\text{km}$.
- **Area** is the amount of flat surface covered. It is measured in **square units**: $\text{cm}^2$, $\text{m}^2$.
- **Volume** is the amount of space occupied. It is measured in **cubic units**: $\text{cm}^3$, $\text{m}^3$.

> **Why the units change:** A length is one-dimensional. Area multiplies two lengths, so the unit is multiplied by itself. Volume multiplies three lengths, so the unit is cubed.

### Converting square and cubic units

Because both dimensions change, square and cubic conversions are not the same as length conversions:

$$
1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}
$$

but

$$
1\text{ m}^2=(100\text{ cm})^2=10{,}000\text{ cm}^2
$$

and

$$
1\text{ m}^3=(100\text{ cm})^3=1{,}000{,}000\text{ cm}^3
$$

### Basic measurement formulas

| Shape or solid | Formula |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | $P=2(l+w)$, $A=lw$ |
| Square | $P=4s$, $A=s^2$ |
| Triangle | $P=a+b+c$, $A=\frac{1}{2}bh$ |
| Circle | $C=2\pi r$, $A=\pi r^2$ |
| Cuboid | $V=lwh$, surface area $=2(lw+lh+wh)$ |
| Cube | $V=s^3$, surface area $=6s^2$ |
| Cylinder | $V=\pi r^2h$, curved surface area $=2\pi rh$ |

Here $b$ is the perpendicular base, $h$ is the perpendicular height, and $r$ is the radius.

### Speed, distance and time

$$
\text{speed}=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}
$$

Keep the units consistent. For example, a speed in $\text{km/h}$ must be paired with a distance in $\text{km}$ and a time in hours.

## 3. How to Solve

### Example 1: Convert length

**Question:** Convert $3.5\text{ m}$ to centimetres.

**Step 1 — Use $1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}$.**

$$
3.5\times100=\boxed{350\text{ cm}}
$$

### Example 2: Convert mass

**Question:** Convert $2.4\text{ kg}$ to grams.

$$
2.4\times1000=\boxed{2400\text{ g}}
$$

### Example 3: Convert area

**Question:** Convert $0.5\text{ m}^2$ to $\text{cm}^2$.

**Step 1 — Use $1\text{ m}^2=10{,}000\text{ cm}^2$.**

$$
0.5\times10{,}000=\boxed{5000\text{ cm}^2}
$$

### Example 4: Area with mixed units

**Question:** Find the area of a rectangle measuring $1.2\text{ m}$ by $50\text{ cm}$.

**Step 1 — Convert to the same unit.** $1.2\text{ m}=120\text{ cm}$.

**Step 2 — Multiply.**

$$
A=120\times50=\boxed{6000\text{ cm}^2}
$$

This is also $0.6\text{ m}^2$.

### Example 5: Volume of a cuboid

**Question:** A box is $20\text{ cm}$ long, $15\text{ cm}$ wide and $10\text{ cm}$ high. Find its volume.

$$
V=lwh=20\times15\times10=\boxed{3000\text{ cm}^3}
$$

### Example 6: Speed

**Question:** A car travels $150\text{ km}$ in $2.5$ hours. Find its average speed.

$$
\text{speed}=\frac{150}{2.5}=\boxed{60\text{ km/h}}
$$

### Example 7: Cost per unit

**Question:** $2.5\text{ kg}$ of rice costs ₹$180$. Find the cost per kilogram.

$$
\frac{180}{2.5}=\boxed{₹72\text{ per kg}}
$$

### Example 8: Convert a long distance downwards

**Question:** Convert $2.75\text{ km}$ to centimetres.

**Step 1 — Use $1\text{ km}=100{,}000\text{ cm}$.**

$$
2.75\times100{,}000=\boxed{275{,}000\text{ cm}}
$$

### Example 9: Convert upwards

**Question:** Convert $640\text{ cm}$ to metres.

$$
640\div100=\boxed{6.4\text{ m}}
$$

### Example 10: Change speed units

**Question:** A car travels at $54\text{ km/h}$. Find its speed in $\text{m/s}$.

$$
54\times\frac{5}{18}=3\times5=\boxed{15\text{ m/s}}
$$

### Example 11: Distance, speed and time with minutes

**Question:** A train travels $90\text{ km}$ at $72\text{ km/h}$. How long does the journey
take, in minutes?

**Step 1 — Keep the units consistent.** Speed is in $\text{km/h}$, distance in km, so
the time comes out in hours.

$$
t=\frac{d}{s}=\frac{90}{72}=1.25\text{ h}
$$

**Step 2 — Convert hours to minutes.**

$$
1.25\times60=\boxed{75\text{ minutes}}
$$

## 4. Watch Out!

| Mistake | Why it is wrong | Correct method |
|---|---|---|
| Writing $1\text{ m}^2=100\text{ cm}^2$ | Both dimensions change. | $1\text{ m}^2=10{,}000\text{ cm}^2$ |
| Mixing $\text{cm}^2$ and $\text{cm}^3$ | Area is two-dimensional; volume is three-dimensional. | Match the unit to the quantity. |
| Multiplying $1.2\text{ m}$ by $50\text{ cm}$ directly | The units differ. | Convert first. |
| Writing area as $\text{cm}$ | Area needs square units. | Write $\text{cm}^2$. |
| Using slant height as perpendicular height | The area formula needs the perpendicular height. | Identify the $90^\circ$ height. |
| Forgetting to convert minutes to hours in a speed problem | The units must match. | $30\text{ min}=0.5\text{ h}$. |
| Multiplying when going from a smaller unit to a bigger unit | The number must get smaller. | $250\text{ cm}=2.5\text{ m}$, not $25{,}000\text{ m}$. |
| Using $\times\frac{18}{5}$ for $\text{km/h}\to\text{m/s}$ | The fraction is the wrong way round. | $\text{km/h}\to\text{m/s}$ uses $\times\frac{5}{18}$. |
| Converting $\text{cm}^2\to\text{m}^2$ by dividing by $100$ | Both dimensions change. | Divide by $10{,}000$. |

> **Examination tip:** Write the unit at every step of a mensuration answer. If the unit is wrong, the numerical answer is usually wrong too.

## 5. Practise and Check

1. Convert $4.8\text{ m}$ to centimetres.
2. Convert $750\text{ g}$ to kilograms.
3. Convert $2.5\text{ L}$ to millilitres.
4. Convert $3\text{ m}^2$ to $\text{cm}^2$.
5. Find the area of a rectangle $2.5\text{ m}$ by $80\text{ cm}$.
6. Find the volume of a cuboid with dimensions $12\text{ cm}$, $8\text{ cm}$ and $5\text{ cm}$.
7. Find the surface area of a cube of side $6\text{ cm}$.
8. A train travels $240\text{ km}$ in $3$ hours. Find its average speed.
9. $1.5\text{ kg}$ of apples costs ₹$210$. Find the cost per kilogram.
10. Convert $90$ minutes to hours.
11. Convert $6.4\text{ km}$ to metres.
12. Convert $350\text{ cm}$ to metres.
13. Convert $72\text{ km/h}$ to $\text{m/s}$.
14. Convert $15\text{ m/s}$ to $\text{km/h}$.
15. Write $45$ minutes as a fraction of an hour.

**Answers**

1. $480\text{ cm}$
2. $0.75\text{ kg}$
3. $2500\text{ mL}$
4. $30{,}000\text{ cm}^2$
5. $2.5\text{ m}=250\text{ cm}$, so $A=250\times80=20{,}000\text{ cm}^2=2\text{ m}^2$
6. $480\text{ cm}^3$
7. $6\times6^2=216\text{ cm}^2$
8. $80\text{ km/h}$
9. ₹$140$ per kg
10. $1.5$ hours
11. $6400\text{ m}$
12. $3.5\text{ m}$
13. $72\times\frac{5}{18}=20\text{ m/s}$
14. $15\times\frac{18}{5}=54\text{ km/h}$
15. $\frac{45}{60}=\frac{3}{4}$ of an hour, or $0.75\text{ h}$

### Mastery check

You are ready to move on when you can:

- convert length, mass, capacity and time units correctly, in either direction;
- explain why area and volume use different conversions;
- use the basic perimeter, area and volume formulas with consistent units;
- state the speed formula and keep its units consistent;
- change between $\text{km/h}$ and $\text{m/s}$ using $\frac{5}{18}$ and $\frac{18}{5}$.
