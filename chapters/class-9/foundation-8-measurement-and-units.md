## Foundation 8: Measurement and Units

Mensuration marks are easy to lose through unit mistakes. This lesson repairs metric conversions, the meaning of square and cubic units, and the basic measurement formulas before the fuller mensuration chapters.

## 1. Remember the Basics

### Metric conversions

| Length | Mass | Capacity |
|---|---|---|
| $1\text{ km}=1000\text{ m}$ | $1\text{ kg}=1000\text{ g}$ | $1\text{ L}=1000\text{ mL}$ |
| $1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}$ | $1\text{ g}=1000\text{ mg}$ | $1\text{ L}=1000\text{ cm}^3$ |
| $1\text{ cm}=10\text{ mm}$ | $1\text{ tonne}=1000\text{ kg}$ | $1\text{ m}^3=1000\text{ L}$ |

### Time conversions

$$
1\text{ hour}=60\text{ minutes},\qquad 1\text{ minute}=60\text{ seconds}
$$

$$
1\text{ day}=24\text{ hours}
$$

### Perimeter, area and volume

- **Perimeter** is the distance around a shape. It is measured in ordinary units: $\text{cm}$, $\text{m}$, $\text{km}$.
- **Area** is the amount of flat surface covered. It is measured in **square units**: $\text{cm}^2$, $\text{m}^2$.
- **Volume** is the amount of space occupied. It is measured in **cubic units**: $\text{cm}^3$, $\text{m}^3$.

> **Why the units change:** A length is one-dimensional. Area multiplies two lengths, so the unit is multiplied by itself. Volume multiplies three lengths, so the unit is cubed.

### Converting square and cubic units

Because both dimensions change, square and cubic conversions are not the same as length conversions:

$$
1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}
$$

but

$$
1\text{ m}^2=(100\text{ cm})^2=10{,}000\text{ cm}^2
$$

and

$$
1\text{ m}^3=(100\text{ cm})^3=1{,}000{,}000\text{ cm}^3
$$

### Basic measurement formulas

| Shape or solid | Formula |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | $P=2(l+w)$, $A=lw$ |
| Square | $P=4s$, $A=s^2$ |
| Triangle | $P=a+b+c$, $A=\frac{1}{2}bh$ |
| Circle | $C=2\pi r$, $A=\pi r^2$ |
| Cuboid | $V=lwh$, surface area $=2(lw+lh+wh)$ |
| Cube | $V=s^3$, surface area $=6s^2$ |
| Cylinder | $V=\pi r^2h$, curved surface area $=2\pi rh$ |

Here $b$ is the perpendicular base, $h$ is the perpendicular height, and $r$ is the radius.

### Speed, distance and time

$$
\text{speed}=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}
$$

Keep the units consistent. For example, a speed in $\text{km/h}$ must be paired with a distance in $\text{km}$ and a time in hours.

## 3. How to Solve

### Example 1: Convert length

**Question:** Convert $3.5\text{ m}$ to centimetres.

**Step 1 — Use $1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}$.**

$$
3.5\times100=\boxed{350\text{ cm}}
$$

### Example 2: Convert mass

**Question:** Convert $2.4\text{ kg}$ to grams.

$$
2.4\times1000=\boxed{2400\text{ g}}
$$

### Example 3: Convert area

**Question:** Convert $0.5\text{ m}^2$ to $\text{cm}^2$.

**Step 1 — Use $1\text{ m}^2=10{,}000\text{ cm}^2$.**

$$
0.5\times10{,}000=\boxed{5000\text{ cm}^2}
$$

### Example 4: Area with mixed units

**Question:** Find the area of a rectangle measuring $1.2\text{ m}$ by $50\text{ cm}$.

**Step 1 — Convert to the same unit.** $1.2\text{ m}=120\text{ cm}$.

**Step 2 — Multiply.**

$$
A=120\times50=\boxed{6000\text{ cm}^2}
$$

This is also $0.6\text{ m}^2$.

### Example 5: Volume of a cuboid

**Question:** A box is $20\text{ cm}$ long, $15\text{ cm}$ wide and $10\text{ cm}$ high. Find its volume.

$$
V=lwh=20\times15\times10=\boxed{3000\text{ cm}^3}
$$

### Example 6: Speed

**Question:** A car travels $150\text{ km}$ in $2.5$ hours. Find its average speed.

$$
\text{speed}=\frac{150}{2.5}=\boxed{60\text{ km/h}}
$$

### Example 7: Cost per unit

**Question:** $2.5\text{ kg}$ of rice costs ₹$180$. Find the cost per kilogram.

$$
\frac{180}{2.5}=\boxed{₹72\text{ per kg}}
$$

## 4. Watch Out!

| Mistake | Why it is wrong | Correct method |
|---|---|---|
| Writing $1\text{ m}^2=100\text{ cm}^2$ | Both dimensions change. | $1\text{ m}^2=10{,}000\text{ cm}^2$ |
| Mixing $\text{cm}^2$ and $\text{cm}^3$ | Area is two-dimensional; volume is three-dimensional. | Match the unit to the quantity. |
| Multiplying $1.2\text{ m}$ by $50\text{ cm}$ directly | The units differ. | Convert first. |
| Writing area as $\text{cm}$ | Area needs square units. | Write $\text{cm}^2$. |
| Using slant height as perpendicular height | The area formula needs the perpendicular height. | Identify the $90^\circ$ height. |
| Forgetting to convert minutes to hours in a speed problem | The units must match. | $30\text{ min}=0.5\text{ h}$. |

> **Examination tip:** Write the unit at every step of a mensuration answer. If the unit is wrong, the numerical answer is usually wrong too.

## 5. Practise and Check

1. Convert $4.8\text{ m}$ to centimetres.
2. Convert $750\text{ g}$ to kilograms.
3. Convert $2.5\text{ L}$ to millilitres.
4. Convert $3\text{ m}^2$ to $\text{cm}^2$.
5. Find the area of a rectangle $2.5\text{ m}$ by $80\text{ cm}$.
6. Find the volume of a cuboid with dimensions $12\text{ cm}$, $8\text{ cm}$ and $5\text{ cm}$.
7. Find the surface area of a cube of side $6\text{ cm}$.
8. A train travels $240\text{ km}$ in $3$ hours. Find its average speed.
9. $1.5\text{ kg}$ of apples costs ₹$210$. Find the cost per kilogram.
10. Convert $90$ minutes to hours.

**Answers**

1. $480\text{ cm}$
2. $0.75\text{ kg}$
3. $2500\text{ mL}$
4. $30{,}000\text{ cm}^2$
5. $2.5\text{ m}=250\text{ cm}$, so $A=250\times80=20{,}000\text{ cm}^2=2\text{ m}^2$
6. $480\text{ cm}^3$
7. $6\times6^2=216\text{ cm}^2$
8. $80\text{ km/h}$
9. ₹$140$ per kg
10. $1.5$ hours

### Mastery check

She is ready to move on when she can:

- convert length, mass, capacity and time units correctly;
- explain why area and volume use different conversions;
- use the basic perimeter, area and volume formulas with consistent units;
- state the speed formula and keep its units consistent.
