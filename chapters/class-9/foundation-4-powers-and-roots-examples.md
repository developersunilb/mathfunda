# Foundation 4: Worked Examples

Worked examples for **Powers and Roots**, including index laws and perfect powers.

## Example 1: Index laws — multiply powers

**Question:** Simplify $2^3\times2^2$.

**Method:** Same base, add the indices: $a^m\times a^n=a^{m+n}$.

$$
2^3\times2^2=2^{3+2}=2^5=32
$$

$$
\boxed{32}
$$

## Example 2: Index laws — power of a power

**Question:** Simplify $(3^2)^3$.

**Method:** Multiply the indices: $(a^m)^n=a^{m\times n}$.

$$
(3^2)^3=3^{2\times3}=3^6=729
$$

$$
\boxed{729}
$$

## Example 3: Square root of a perfect square

**Question:** Find $\sqrt{144}$.

**Method:** Find the number which, squared, gives 144. Since $12\times12=144$, $\sqrt{144}=12$.

$$
\boxed{12}
$$

## Example 4: Index laws — divide powers

**Question:** Simplify $\frac{5^7}{5^3}$.

**Method:** Same base, subtract the indices: $\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}$.

$$
\frac{5^7}{5^3}=5^{7-3}=5^4=625
$$

$$
\boxed{625}
$$

## Example 5: Spot a perfect power

**Question:** Is $125$ a perfect cube? If so, find its cube root.

**Method:** Test cubes: $5^3=5\times5\times5=125$. So yes, $125=5^3$.

**Step 1 — Check $5^3$.**

$$
5^3=125
$$

**Step 2 — Cube root.**

$$
\sqrt[3]{125}=5
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Yes, }125=5^3,\text{ so }\sqrt[3]{125}=5}
$$
