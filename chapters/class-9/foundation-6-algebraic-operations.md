## Foundation 6: Algebraic Operations

Brackets are where most algebra marks are won or lost. This lesson repairs expansion, simplification and basic factorisation using the distributive property.

## 1. Remember the Basics

### The distributive property

Multiplication distributes over addition and subtraction:

$$
a(b+c)=ab+ac
$$

$$
a(b-c)=ab-ac
$$

This single idea explains every expansion and every algebraic identity.

### Expanding one bracket

$$
3(x+4)=3x+12
$$

Every term inside the bracket is multiplied by the number outside.

### Expanding two brackets

Multiply each term in the first bracket by each term in the second:

$$
(x+2)(x+5)=x(x+5)+2(x+5)
$$

$$
=x^2+5x+2x+10
$$

$$
=x^2+7x+10
$$

A quick pattern for $(x+a)(x+b)$:

$$
(x+a)(x+b)=x^2+(a+b)x+ab
$$

### The three core identities

$$
(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2
$$

$$
(a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2
$$

$$
(a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2
$$

> **Why the middle term appears:** $(a+b)^2=(a+b)(a+b)=a^2+ab+ba+b^2$, and $ab=ba$, so the two middle terms add to $2ab$.

### Factorising by a common factor

Factorising is the reverse of expanding. Take out the highest common factor of every term:

$$
6x+9=3(2x+3)
$$

### Difference of two squares

A subtraction of two perfect squares factorises as

$$
a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)
$$

For example:

$$
x^2-49=(x+7)(x-7)
$$

### Expanding versus factorising

| Direction | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Expand | Brackets are multiplied out | $(x+3)(x+4)=x^2+7x+12$ |
| Factorise | An expression is written as a product | $x^2+7x+12=(x+3)(x+4)$ |

## 2. Formula Bank

| Idea | Formula |
|---|---|
| Distributive property | $a(b+c)=ab+ac$ |
| Subtracting a bracket | $a-(b+c)=a-b-c$ |
| Product of two binomials | $(x+a)(x+b)=x^2+(a+b)x+ab$ |
| Square of a sum | $(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2$ |
| Square of a difference | $(a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2$ |
| Difference of squares | $a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)$ |
| Common factor | $ab+ac=a(b+c)$ |

## 3. How to Solve

### Example 1: Expand and simplify

**Question:** Expand and simplify $4(2x-3)+5$.

**Step 1 — Multiply each term inside the bracket by $4$.**

$$
8x-12+5
$$

**Step 2 — Collect the constants.**

$$
\boxed{8x-7}
$$

### Example 2: Expand two brackets

**Question:** Expand $(x+3)(x+5)$.

**Step 1 — Multiply each term in the first bracket by each term in the second.**

$$
x(x+5)+3(x+5)
$$

**Step 2 — Expand.**

$$
x^2+5x+3x+15
$$

**Step 3 — Collect like terms.**

$$
\boxed{x^2+8x+15}
$$

### Example 3: Square a binomial

**Question:** Expand $(x+3)^2$.

**Step 1 — Identify $a=x$ and $b=3$.**

**Step 2 — Apply $(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2$.**

$$
x^2+2(x)(3)+3^2
$$

**Step 3 — Simplify.**

$$
\boxed{x^2+6x+9}
$$

### Example 4: Subtract a bracket

**Question:** Simplify $5x-(2x-7)$.

**Step 1 — The minus sign changes the sign of every term in the bracket.**

$$
5x-2x+7
$$

**Step 2 — Collect like terms.**

$$
\boxed{3x+7}
$$

### Example 5: Factorise a common factor

**Question:** Factorise $12x^2-18x$.

**Step 1 — Find the HCF of $12x^2$ and $18x$.** The HCF is $6x$.

**Step 2 — Write each term as the HCF times the remaining factor.**

$$
12x^2-18x=6x(2x-3)
$$

**Step 3 — Check by expanding.**

$$
6x(2x-3)=12x^2-18x
$$

### Example 6: Difference of squares

**Question:** Factorise $x^2-49$.

**Step 1 — Recognise $49=7^2$.**

$$
x^2-49=x^2-7^2
$$

**Step 2 — Apply $a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)$.**

$$
\boxed{(x+7)(x-7)}
$$

## 4. Watch Out!

| Mistake | Why it is wrong | Correct method |
|---|---|---|
| $(x+3)^2=x^2+9$ | The middle term is missing. | $x^2+6x+9$ |
| $4(2x-3)=8x-3$ | The $4$ multiplies both terms. | $8x-12$ |
| $5x-(2x-7)=5x-2x-7$ | The minus sign applies to both terms. | $5x-2x+7$ |
| $x\cdot x=2x$ | Multiplication of $x$ by $x$ gives a square. | $x^2$ |
| $2x+3x=5x^2$ | Like terms add coefficients; the variable does not change. | $5x$ |
| Factorising $x^2+9$ as $(x+3)^2$ | There is no middle term matching the identity. | $x^2+9$ does not factorise this way over the integers. |

> **Examination tip:** After factorising, expand mentally to check. If the expansion does not return the original expression, the factorisation is wrong.

## 5. Practise and Check

1. Expand $3(x+5)$.
2. Expand and simplify $2(3x-4)+7$.
3. Expand $(x+4)(x+2)$.
4. Expand $(x-5)(x+3)$.
5. Expand $(x+6)^2$.
6. Expand $(x-4)^2$.
7. Expand $(x+9)(x-9)$.
8. Simplify $7x-(3x-5)$.
9. Factorise $8x+12$.
10. Factorise $15x^2-25x$.
11. Factorise $x^2-64$.
12. Factorise $x^2+9x+20$.

**Answers**

1. $3x+15$
2. $6x-1$
3. $x^2+6x+8$
4. $x^2-2x-15$
5. $x^2+12x+36$
6. $x^2-8x+16$
7. $x^2-81$
8. $4x+5$
9. $4(2x+3)$
10. $5x(3x-5)$
11. $(x+8)(x-8)$
12. $(x+4)(x+5)$

### Mastery check

She is ready to move on when she can:

- expand one and two brackets without dropping a term;
- handle a minus sign before a bracket;
- use the three core identities correctly;
- factorise by a common factor and by the difference of squares.
