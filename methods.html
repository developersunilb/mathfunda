THE MEMORY METHOD

Remember more
by studying less.

Most students revise by rereading. It feels productive because the page is familiar, but familiarity is not memory. These five methods are the ones with the strongest evidence behind them — and they are built into this site.

1. Retrieval practice

Close the book and try to produce the answer. The effort, not the rereading, builds the memory.

2. Spaced repetition

Review just before you would forget. Each successful recall pushes the next review further away.

3. Interleaving

Mix topics in one session. It feels harder than blocked practice, and it transfers better to exams.

4. Elaboration

Ask “why is this true?” and connect it to something you already know.

5. Worked examples, then fading

Study a solved example, then redo it with one line hidden, then from scratch.

6. Sleep and spacing

Six short sessions across six days beat one long session. Sleep does part of the consolidation.

How the flashcard schedule works

Every card has a box number. A correct “Good” answer moves it up a box; a miss sends it back to the first box. The intervals grow: 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 16 days, 35 days. A card you rate “Again” comes back in ten minutes and again at the end of the session.

RatingMeaningWhat happens
AgainI could not recall it.Back to box 1, ten minutes, and re-queued today.
HardI got there, slowly.Drop one box; a shorter interval.
GoodCorrect with a little effort.Move up one box; the normal interval.
EasyImmediate and confident.Move up two boxes; a long interval.

Be honest with the rating. Rating everything “Easy” feels good and ruins the schedule. The aim is to meet a card when it is slightly hard to recall.

How to learn a formula, not just read it

  1. Say what each letter means. $A=\frac{1}{2}bh$ is only useful if you know $h$ is perpendicular height.
  2. Write it from memory on blank paper, then compare.
  3. Use it once with easy numbers, then once with fractions or negatives.
  4. Explain when it does not apply. That is where marks are lost.
  5. Connect it to a neighbour. Heron's formula becomes the triangle area formula when you substitute the height.

Memorising the reference values

The roots, squares and $\pi$ values live in Foundation 4 and Foundation 8. Learn the anchors, not the whole list:

  • $\sqrt{2}\approx1.414$, $\sqrt{3}\approx1.732$, $\sqrt{5}\approx2.236$.
  • $\sqrt{8}=2\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt{3}$, $\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt{5}$.
  • $\pi\approx3.14$ or $\frac{22}{7}$; use $\frac{22}{7}$ when the radius is a multiple of 7.
  • Squares to $20^2$ and cubes to $10^3$, read in both directions.
  • $\pi$ radians $=180^\circ$, so $\frac{\pi}{2}=90^\circ$ and $\frac{\pi}{3}=60^\circ$.

Why the pages feel small

Each lesson is broken into book-sized pages automatically. You see one page at a time, which keeps the task finite. Turn the page only when you have actually read the current one. If a page feels dense, slow down: the page count is not a deadline.

The one rule that matters most: if you can explain it out loud without looking, you know it. If you can only recognise it on the page, you do not yet.
